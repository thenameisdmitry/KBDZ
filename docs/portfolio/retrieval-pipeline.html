Retrieval Pipeline, Stage by Stage

The assistant shows what retrieval does. This shows where it breaks, and who fixes each break.

Question

Can I change the currency of an account later?

The customer asks

Input
Free text typed into a support widget
Output
"Can I change the currency of an account later?"

Nothing is known yet about the product, the version or the customer’s plan. Anything the pipeline needs to disambiguate the question has to come from metadata or from the documentation itself.

Fixed by the writer

The question uses the customer’s vocabulary, not yours. If your heading says "Immutable attributes" and the customer says "change the currency", the lexical stage has nothing to match.

Why the ownership labels matter​

When an agent answers wrongly, the reflex is to treat it as a model problem: swap the embedding model, raise the top-k, widen the context window.

Five of the seven stages fail for reasons that live in the documentation:

StageThe writer's share
QueryHeadings use internal vocabulary instead of the customer's
ChunkA fact depends on the paragraph above it and does not survive the split
RetrieveThe answer exists but shares no wording with the question
GenerateThe chunks do not contain the answer, so the model composes one
CiteThe cited section does not support the claim

Only embedding and reranking are genuinely infrastructure. No amount of tuning recovers a fact that was never written in a findable place.

The stage worth staring at​

Generate. A model handed three passages that do not answer the question almost never says so. It produces a fluent paragraph in the register of your documentation, with your terminology, and the customer cannot tell it apart from a real answer.

The dangerous output is not the blank one. It is the confident one, which is why RAG Evaluation measures answers rather than trusting prose.
